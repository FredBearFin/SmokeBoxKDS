# Exit gates

Four gates after one observed service. They decide whether to keep building for barbecue pit kitchens, not whether the product works. One service in one kitchen can say "no" much better than "yes". Any single failure means stop and think before building more.

Where the numbers come from: the app's **Pilot report** (Back office, Menu and settings, Data, group "Spreadsheet files and the pilot report": choose a window, type **Cuts counted**, tap **Show report** or **Download CSV**), plus the observer sheet, plus two spoken questions. A3 is changing the report so it matches these gates (a second count box, a Gates block, and DEMO stamping); this page quotes only what the report says today and must be checked against it once that lands.

## Gate 1: the owner can name a waste cost worth the price

- **Pass:** the owner names what they threw out last week and its cost, and the figure is at least one month's price.
- **Measured by:** a spoken question, asked after the service, exact words written on the sheet ("What did you throw out last week, and what did it cost?"). The Pilot report line "Discards: N batches, grams, cost" shows what the app logged and a rough week figure; it is a guess from one service and is not the owner's number. The cost is only as good as the cost per kg the owner entered.
- **If it fails:** the waste value is thin for this kind of place, or the owner does not watch it. Rethink who this is for before building anything. Do not lower the price to make it pass.

## Gate 2: at least half of the real cuts get logged

- **Pass:** the Pilot report says at least 50 percent of the cuts you counted got a Pull tap.
- **Measured by:** your cut tally, typed into **Cuts counted**, against the app's Pull taps. The report line reads "Observer counted N cuts: X percent got a pull tap (the gate needs at least 50)". Cuts nobody tapped are invisible to the app, which is why you count by eye.
- **If it fails:** cuts are not being logged, so any run-out estimate is weak. First look at where the tablet sits and who is meant to tap it. Do not build more projection features. If the owner says logging is too much work, that is the finding.

## Gate 3: warnings are heard from the pit

- **Pass:** you heard each warning from the pit position without looking at the screen, and the cook acted on it, in most cases.
- **Measured by:** the observer sheet only. The app records that a sound played and whether someone tapped the warning. It cannot know a warning was heard. The report's "Sounds" line is the app's side of the story; your table is the answer.
- **If it fails:** try a speaker, a different position, or a visual-only plan before anything else. If sleep or a hidden screen lost a warning, fix that (an always-on tablet) or stop.

## Gate 4: the owner would pay something

- **Pass:** the owner says an amount they would pay each month, at or near the price you wrote down.
- **Measured by:** a spoken question, exact words on the sheet. You quote the price first, then stop talking.
- **If it fails:** the positioning is wrong. Stop and rethink. Do not argue, discount or promise features.

## Reported but not pass or fail

Pull taps by hour, discards by reason, warnings that were wrong or missing (in the cooks' words), things cooks ignored, ticket time if tickets were used, and anything that broke. Keep the CSV and photos.

## What the app cannot measure

Cuts nobody tapped, food that never went through the app, whether a warning was heard, whether the owner would pay, and how long setup really took.

## If all four pass

The idea survived one service in one kitchen. Next: a second kitchen, then decide on anything bigger. It does not mean the product is proven, and it says nothing about compliance.
